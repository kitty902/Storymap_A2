Where In New York City Are Inspection Results Most Often Critical?

An exploration of who really is affected by poor restaurant operations by comparing population density, median household income, and official restaurant inspection results.

§

Introduction

Known as the “The Big Apple”, New York City (NYC) is recognized for its fast-paced lifestyle, cultural diversity, and vibrant food scene. With an abundance of restaurants representing cuisines from around the world, restaurants are an essential part of everyday life for many New Yorkers. With approximately one-third of their calorie intake coming from foods consumed outside the home, New Yorkers must be conscious of the quality and safety of the food they consume (Wong et al., 2015). As residents spend a significant portion of their day away from home, restaurant cleanliness, food safety, and access to quality food becomes important.

Given NYC’s extensive restaurant industry across its boroughs, it is important to explore and understand the restaurant inspection results and compare them between the boroughs. Our study examines the relationship between socioeconomic and demographic characteristics, particularly the median household income and population distribution, and the restaurant inspection results managed by the NYC Department of Health and Mental Hygiene (DOHMH).

Using the restaurant inspection data along with the income and population data for each borough, we will investigate the geography distribution of critical and non-critical violations and compare average inspection scores across different cuisine categories and boroughs. Through these comparisons, we aim to identify patterns and explore whether differences in the inspection results are associated with socioeconomic characteristics of the boroughs in which they operate in. Understanding these patterns can help identify the areas that may require greater attentions from public health authorities, inform food safety policies, encourage a safer food environment across NYC, and critically evaluate the US healthcare system.

Final Takeaway

Conclusion

Our data exploration highlights Staten Island as performing significantly worse in restaurant food inspections especially places serving Latin American cuisine.This is interesting as the borough is lowest in density and wealth seems to play no significant role. However, despite all this we want our analysis to encourage the consumer to check official health & food inspection results on the NYC ABCEats site and familiarize themselves with the New York letter grading system; rather simply trusting social media. It is important to note influencers may not understand or recognize what goes on beyond the counter, but official health inspectors do. The US Healthcare system is costly and many may choose to avoid visiting to save on the possibility of a considerably large bill at the expense of their health. When restaurants are often operating under insanitary conditions in lower income neighbourhoods such as those infrequent tracts within Staten Island, all of Bronx and Southern Queens; an invisible type of injustice is produced where such populations are more likely to fall sick and question themselves on whether they should spend a significant amount on healthcare.This is especially dangerous for those more vulnerable including young children, pregnant women, the elderly and those with a weaker immune system.
